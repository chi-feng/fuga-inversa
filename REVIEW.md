# Review and response

The reviews led to revisions in the analysis, notation annotations, page layout, and web player. The canonical musical source, score MIDI and performing PDF, final recordings, and native 24-bit piano inputs retain their initial publication hashes. The analytical PDF and web presentation have changed. Fable's focused follow-up reported no material regression; its remaining findings and their dispositions are recorded below.

Claude Fable 5.1 (`claude-fable-5-1`) reviewed the initial published commit [7a156b0deea04743f2fc06264f6162813388e709](https://github.com/chi-feng/fuga-inversa/commit/7a156b0deea04743f2fc06264f6162813388e709) on 7 September 2026. GPT-6 Astra agents composed the fugue and produced the edition in this session. The reviewer therefore came from a different model family. The [complete initial report](reviews/2026-09-07-fable-initial.md) is preserved as a historical document, including the reviewer claims corrected below.

It includes their effect on voice independence among the work's limitations. |

The double-octave approaches at beats 2 and 4 of mm. 13–14 are **contrary, similar, similar, similar**, each with a soprano step. The outer framework alternates **compound thirds and double octaves**. The analysis acknowledges the wide spacing and regular framework; their musical effect remains an interpretive judgment. |
| The final leading-tone motion and spacing invited an examiner's objection. | **Interpretation acknowledged.** The analysis describes C♯4–E4 and C♯5–E5–D5 as an indirect cadential flourish and names the final minor thirteenth between F♯3 and D5. The review did not establish a rule fault or an examiner's actual judgment. The score remains unchanged. |

In the revised [analytical PDF](assets/pdf/fuga-inversa-analysis.pdf), Example 6 remains before the tonic-return section; the references and appendix no longer require a nearly empty intervening page. |
| Eager decoding and retained buffers could exhaust a phone's audio memory. | **Resource use reduced; device hypothesis open.** The [player](source/player.ts) loads the master for all voices or only the selected stems for a subset. It drops obsolete cached buffers, cancels superseded fetches, and discards late results. It requests 48 kHz, with a device-default fallback only when that rate is unsupported. Chrome confirmed 48 kHz and held about 28.74 MB for the piano master, 57.48 MB for two stems, and 61.54 MB while repeating that pair. These figures cover held PCM, not total browser memory. No physical iPhone failure was established or ruled out. |
| Switching instruments might produce a loudness jump. Abrupt pauses and repeat boundaries might click. | **Measured and treated.** The source MP3 masters measured −17.99 LUFS for organ and −20.00 LUFS for piano. Enhanced playback applies −2.01 dB to the organ master and its stems. Native fallback and downloads retain their original levels. Pauses use a 10 ms fade; private repeat copies use 5 ms endpoint tapers. The browser checks confirm that the first and last samples of each loop copy are zero, as the taper requires. Audibility and perceived smoothness remain untested. |
| The stretto comparison silently changed the global voice selection. The main voice controls also blurred toggling and soloing. | **Accepted.** The [comparison callout](edition/includes/stretto-callout.html) and player status explain that the selection persists. The main controls explicitly switch voices on or off; excerpt controls use “Solo.” “Whole piece” is also available in the floating player while an excerpt remains selected, including after a pause. |
| Missing Web Audio or an initial data-load failure left visible controls inert. | **Accepted.** The [native playback helper](source/native-playback.ts) initializes before the enhancement data loads. Instrument selection updates the native source and its metadata. Unavailable playback controls stay hidden, and the status explains the fallback. Score-only voice controls use “Highlight.” Missing-AudioContext and failed-data requests have separate browser checks. |
| Small control text, low-contrast inactive voices, missing group roles, and inconsistent PDF labels reduced accessibility. | **Accepted.** Status, navigation, metadata, scroll hints, and Solo labels now use at least 16 px screen text. Inactive voice text measures 5.67:1 against its player background, with a hollow marker and strike-through for the off state. Labelled toolbar and annotation containers have group roles. PDF downloads show “PDF ↓,” and their accessible names include “PDF.” These changes do not establish complete accessibility conformance. |
| The phone hero engraving was too small, the full-score image was soft, and web code did not use Inconsolata. | **Accepted.** Below 760 px the hero engraving is hidden while the pitch names remain visible. Analytical excerpts retain horizontal scrolling. The full-score image has a 2× source with unchanged proportions. A self-hosted, unmodified Inconsolata font and its SIL Open Font License notice support the web code block, with upright ASCII quotes and a 16 px minimum. |
| Unused excerpts, duplicate prose, CSS overrides, and unused timeline note arrays increased maintenance and payload size. | **Accepted.** The unused G-entry range and public files were removed, leaving eight analytical figures. The duplicate `classical-analysis.md` was removed. CSS overrides were consolidated into the existing layers. Physical Safari/iPhone timing, output latency, and memory pressure have not been tested. The initial review's Safari timing claim remains a hypothesis. |

## Evidence and remaining limits

The response was checked against the current LilyPond source, source/MIDI and excerpt comparisons, browser-player source, numerical audio measurements, muted Chrome functional and stress checks, fallback checks, and the regenerated paper proofs. The unchanged musical and recording files were compared with the initial publication audit's SHA256 values. The revised analytical PDF is a separate artifact.

The published checks retain their methods and limits:

- [Pitch, motion, and excerpt comparisons](reviews/evidence/musical-triage.json).
- [Playback and voice identity](reviews/evidence/browser-functional.json), [rapid playback changes](reviews/evidence/browser-stress.json), and [interrupted comparison loads](reviews/evidence/comparison-interruption.json).

- [Source identities, regression results, and current build checks](reviews/evidence/validation.json), with [browser check instructions](reviews/checks/README.md).

The initial reviewer did not audition the recordings, perform a keyboard trial, load the live site, execute the scripts, or reach the external citations. The subsequent browser checks used muted output. The production and review agents therefore make no claim of a final audition, physical keyboard trial, or demonstrated concert-recording realism. Numerical fades and timing checks cannot settle those questions.

The coordinating agent also found a cancellation fault after the initial review. Native playback or a page-hide event could stop the player while a pending stretto preset later restarted it. The [application](source/app.ts) now invalidates the preset's awaited continuation for both events. The two [regression tests](tests/ui-controls.test.mjs) failed before that change and pass after it. A subsequent Chrome check held two selected-stem loads pending, then either started muted native playback or dispatched `pagehide`. Both cases finished with zero late excerpt starts and no remaining sources. This correction came from the coordinator's inspection, not from Fable's report.
